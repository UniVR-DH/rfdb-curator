# Language in anything published

These govern what **you** write: documentation, commit messages, comments, copy.
They do **not** license rewriting text someone else authored. A quoted abstract,
a paper title, a partner's statement: those are their words. Leave them alone
even where they break these rules.

- **No em dashes unless the sentence needs one and no alternative exists.** Try
  a comma, a colon, parentheses, or two sentences first. One of them almost
  always works, and reads faster. The bar is not "a dash reads well here", it is
  "nothing else will do".
- **Name the specific thing.** Prefer the technical term to the general one: "a
  large language model", "a coding agent", "a generative image tool" rather than
  "AI". "AI" is acceptable only where the breadth is the point, or where it is
  the term the source itself uses.
- **No buzzwords and no marketing register.** No "leverage", "cutting-edge",
  "seamless", "powerful", "revolutionise", "unlock". If a sentence would fit in
  a product launch, rewrite it.
- **Favour the source.** Link the authority rather than restating it, and quote
  a figure only where it is published. Between paraphrasing a record and linking
  it, link it.
- **Say it once.** If a fact belongs in two places, put it in the one a reader
  will look in and link from the other. Repetition is how documentation goes
  stale in one copy and not the other.
- **Be brief.** Cut any sentence that survives only to soften another one. No
  preamble, no restating the question, no summary of what you just said.
- **Write documentation as what to do, not as what is not needed.** "Run
  `npm ci` first" beats "no global install required". A prerequisites section
  lists prerequisites and nothing else.
- **Inclusive terminology**, following the ACM's *Words Matter* guidance:
  <https://www.acm.org/diversity-inclusion/words-matter>. Prefer the neutral
  term where one exists: allowlist/blocklist, primary/replica, placeholder name
  rather than "dummy".
